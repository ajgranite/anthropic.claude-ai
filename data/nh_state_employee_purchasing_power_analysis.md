# NH State Employee Purchasing Power Loss Analysis (2023-2026)

## Executive Summary

Despite receiving a 12.2% cumulative raise in 2023-2024, NH state employees have experienced **ongoing purchasing power erosion** due to:
1. Wages that were already **14% behind** before the 2023 raises
2. **No raises since July 2024** (18+ months) while inflation continues
3. Housing costs rising **far faster** than general CPI
4. Northeast region inflation running **higher than national average**

**Bottom Line: NH state employees have lost approximately 5-7% purchasing power since their last raise in July 2024, and remain structurally behind market rates.**

## Purchasing Power Analysis

### Scenario: $60,000 State Employee (Jan 2023 baseline)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Starting salary (Jan 2023) | $60,000 |
| After 10% raise (July 2023) | $66,000 |
| After 2% raise (July 2024) | $67,320 |
| Current salary (Jan 2026) | $67,320 |
| **Salary needed to match inflation** | **$67,020** |

At first glance, the 12.2% raise slightly outpaced the 11.7% cumulative CPI.

### BUT: The Critical Missing Context

#### 1. Wages Were Already 14% Behind

> "Wages for state employees are behind by 14 percent."
> — Rich Gulla, SEA/SEIU Local 1984 President (2023)

The 12.2% raise was meant to **close a pre-existing gap**, not get ahead.

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Pre-2023 wage gap | -14% |
| 2023-2024 raises | +12.2% |
| **Net position** | **Still -1.8% behind** |

#### 2. No Raises Since July 2024 (18 Months of Erosion)

Since the last raise in July 2024:

| Period | Inflation | Wage Increase | Net |
|--------|-----------|---------------|-----|
| July 2024 - Dec 2024 | ~1.75% | 0% | -1.75% |
| Jan 2025 - Dec 2025 | ~3.3% | 0% | -3.3% |
| Jan 2026 | ~0.25% | 0% | -0.25% |
| **Total since last raise** | **~5.3%** | **0%** | **-5.3%** |

**Purchasing power loss since July 2024: approximately 5.3%**

#### 3. Housing Costs Far Exceed CPI

General CPI significantly **understates** the true cost of living increase for workers:

| Cost Category | Increase Since 2015 |
|--------------|---------------------|
| General CPI | ~30% |
| **Housing costs** | **113%** |

For workers spending 30-40% of income on housing, the **effective inflation rate is much higher** than headline CPI.

## Key Arguments for Wage/COLA Increase

### 1. Purchasing Power Is Eroding Again

- **5.3% loss** since July 2024 with no raise
- By July 2025, it will be 2 full years since a raise
- By then, cumulative erosion will exceed **6-7%**

### 2. Original Raise Didn't Close the Gap

- Wages were 14% behind in 2023
- 12.2% raise left workers **still behind**
- The "historic" raise was catch-up, not advancement

### 3. Housing Crisis Hits Harder Than CPI Shows

- Housing up **113%** since 2015
- CPI only up ~30% in same period
- Workers face **real** cost increases 3-4x higher than CPI for their largest expense

### 4. Northeast Inflation Exceeds National Average

- 2025 Northeast: **3.3%** vs. national 2.7%
- NH workers face **22% higher inflation** than national average
- Federal COLA based on national CPI is insufficient

### 5. Cost of NOT Raising Wages

- 19% vacancy rate
- $11.5M in temporary staffing (tripled from $3.8M)
- Retention bonuses of $5,000 already needed
- 6+ month job openings

## Recommended COLA/Wage Increase

To restore purchasing power and begin closing the private sector gap:

| Scenario | Increase Needed |
|----------|-----------------|
| Restore July 2024 purchasing power | 5.3% |
| Close remaining pre-2023 gap | +1.8% |
| **Minimum catch-up increase** | **7.1%** |
| Begin closing private sector gap | +5-10% additional |
| **Competitive wage increase** | **12-17%** |
